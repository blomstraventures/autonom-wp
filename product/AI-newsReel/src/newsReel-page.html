The problem

Why do most AI news Shorts fail?

They write fluently and verify nothing. That is what gets channels ignored, corrected in the comments or demonetized.

!

Invented facts

A model asked to "write a Short about this headline" fills the gaps from memory: a wrong number, a made-up cause, a quote nobody said. One viral mistake costs more trust than a hundred videos earn.

!

Sameness at volume

YouTube's inauthentic content policy targets repetitive, mass-produced videos with no original input. Template clones with swapped nouns are exactly what it describes.

!

Hours of assembly

Finding the story, reading it, writing, recording a voice, hunting footage, syncing captions, exporting. Work that should take minutes eats the day.

Workflow

How does Autonom Newsreel work?

In eight steps that follow the order a good editor works in. Each step is a checkpoint you control.

  1. 1

    Channel

    Read your last public videos from YouTube, or define your channel by hand. Autonom derives topics, scan signals, tone and format. Add your brand links and edit topics and weights yourself.

  2. 2

    Feed

    Scans publisher feeds and news search against your signals. Each story is scored for relevance and freshness, and marked readable or paywalled before you spend anything on it.

  3. 3

    Consolidate

    Groups stories about the same event from different outlets, up to three, so you get one script instead of three near-duplicates. Move or remove stories, and let it run in the background.

  4. 4

    Generate

    Autonom reads the full article, extracts facts with their quotes, checks them, then writes the script. Stories with no readable source are skipped, not guessed.

  5. 5

    Review

    Every script is a card. Edit the hook or analysis, see its sources and checks, adjust hashtags, delete what you do not want, and undo if you slip. Mark it reviewed when you are happy.

  6. 6

    Video

    Choose a voice. Autonom narrates each sentence, picks stock footage scene by scene, burns in word-timed captions and records the video in your browser. Edit any scene and re-record.

  7. 7

    Publish

    A caption written for each platform, ready to copy. Post directly to Telegram or Mastodon with your own token, and plan a publish time per video.

  8. 8

    Export

    One ZIP with the video, audio, SRT captions, script, a ready-to-paste title, description and tags, sources and footage credits. Upload with the disclosure settings you choose.

Trust

How does it keep scripts factual?

By requiring a readable source and tying every fact to a quote from it. These checks run before a script reaches you.

What it checks

  • A readable source is required. No article, no script.
  • Every fact carries a quote from the article, and the claim is compared with that quote.
  • Numbers and names must appear in the source. Unsupported figures are removed.
  • Second-outlet corroboration compares the same event across two publishers when one is available.
  • Analysis is held to a stricter rule: no new numbers, no forecasts stated as fact, no absolutes like "always" or "never".
  • Paywalled story? Autonom searches open outlets for the same event, writes from that article, and says so on the card.

What it will not do

  • Write from a headline alone.
  • Present a paywalled article as read when it was not.
  • Invent a quote, a statistic or a cause.
  • Publish anything without your approval.
  • Guarantee that a source itself is right. Autonom checks that the script matches the article, not that the article is true. Corroboration helps, your judgment decides.
Studio

What does the built-in video studio do?

It builds the finished video in your browser from the script you approved. No separate editor and no render queue.

Natural voice, no recording

A neural text-to-speech model runs on your own computer. Pick a voice and speed, and each sentence is spoken with exact timing so captions stay in sync. The first run downloads the voice model once, then your browser keeps it.

Footage that matches the script

The scene planner chooses what each moment needs, then searches Pixabay or Pexels with your key and picks the best clip. Edit any scene and re-record. Where footage would mislead, it uses a clean text card.

Captions and a full package

Large, word-timed captions are burned in. The export ZIP includes the video, WAV audio, SRT file, script, upload details and footage credits. Videos stay in your browser until you delete them.

Good to know: video is recorded in real time, so a 60 second Short takes about a minute to render. Use a recent Chrome or Edge on a desktop for the best result.

Fit

Who is it for, and will YouTube accept the videos?

It suits channels with a point of view. YouTube does not ban AI-assisted content. It targets repetitive, mass-produced uploads with no original input, and Autonom is designed as an editor's assistant, not a content farm.

Where it fits

  • News, geopolitics, markets and tech commentary channels
  • Newsletters and publishers adding a Shorts format
  • Solo creators who want a few strong videos a day, not fifty weak ones
  • Researchers and analysts who need a sourced summary quickly

How to stay on the right side

  • Set your own angle in the channel profile and edit every script.
  • Publish fewer, better videos. Quality beats volume.
  • Use the AI disclosure setting in YouTube Studio where it applies, for example realistic synthetic imagery. Check YouTube's current policy, it changes.
  • Keep the source named in the description. The export includes it.
Privacy

Is my data and are my API keys private?

Autonom has no accounts and no database. Your profile, scripts, videos and API keys are stored in your browser only.

  • Stored locally: profile, scripts, finished videos, settings and keys, in your browser's storage and unencrypted, so use this on your own device.
  • Relayed, not stored: news search, article fetching, some AI provider calls and stock-footage downloads pass through this website's server, which forwards them and keeps only a short cache of public articles. Your scripts and keys are not saved on it.
  • Direct from your browser: YouTube lookups, Telegram and Mastodon posts, and several AI providers use your own keys.
  • You can verify it. Open DevTools, watch the Network tab, and see exactly where each request goes.

Autonom is not affiliated with YouTube, Telegram, Mastodon, Pixabay, Pexels or any model provider. Provider terms and usage costs depend on your own accounts and plans.

Frequently asked questions

What is Autonom Newsreel?

Autonom Newsreel is a browser-based tool that turns live news into YouTube Shorts. It scans publisher feeds against your channel profile, reads the full article, writes an analytical script where every fact is tied to a quote from the source, and renders a captioned video with an in-browser voice and stock footage. You review everything before anything is posted.

How does Autonom Newsreel turn news into a Short?

It works in eight steps: Channel, Feed, Consolidate, Generate, Review, Video, Publish and Export. It learns your channel, scans news, groups stories about the same event, writes and fact-checks a script from the full article, lets you edit it, builds the captioned video in your browser, prepares captions for each platform and exports a ZIP.

Is Autonom Newsreel free?

The tool is free to use, needs no account and adds no watermark. You bring your own API keys, so any cost comes from your plans with AI and footage providers, not from Autonom. Check each provider's current terms and limits.

Does it make up facts?

It is built not to. Each fact is extracted with a quote from the article, compared against that quote, and checked for numbers and names missing from the source. If there is no readable article, the story is skipped. It cannot tell you whether the article itself is correct, so read each script before you publish.

Which AI providers does it work with?

Google Google Gemini, Groq, OpenRouter, NVIDIA NIM, OpenAI and DeepSeek. You use your own key. Autonom discovers the models available to that key instead of hardcoding names, and falls back to another model when one is rate limited.

Do I need an account or to install anything?

No. Autonom Newsreel runs in a modern desktop browser with no sign-up and no install. Your profile, scripts, finished videos and keys are stored in your own browser. Use recent Chrome or Edge on a desktop for video.

Is my data and are my API keys private?

Your profile, scripts, videos and keys are stored in your browser only, unencrypted, so use it on your own device. Some requests, such as news search, article fetching and a few provider or footage calls, pass through this website's server, which forwards them and keeps only a short cache of public articles. Your scripts and keys are not saved on it.

Can it post or schedule videos for me?

It can post to Telegram channels and Mastodon directly from your browser with your own tokens, and it writes a tailored caption for 11 platforms. Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, X and LinkedIn require approved apps or paid posting services, so for those you download the package or use an automation tool. Direct YouTube upload is not supported today.

Will YouTube demonetize AI-made Shorts?

YouTube's policy targets repetitive, mass-produced content with no original input, not AI assistance as such. Channels that add their own angle, edit every script and publish at a human pace are the opposite of that. Policies change, so read YouTube's current rules and use the AI disclosure setting where it applies.

How is it different from other AI Shorts generators?

Most topic-to-script generators write from a headline or the model's memory and verify nothing. Autonom Newsreel requires a readable source, ties each fact to a quote, skips stories it cannot read, groups the same event from several outlets into one script, and keeps your data in your browser. It checks that a script matches its article, not that the article is true.

Why was my story skipped?

Usually because the article is paywalled, blocked or only an aggregator link. Autonom first searches open outlets for the same event and writes from that article, and says so on the card. If none is found, it skips the story rather than writing from the headline.

Is the voice good enough for YouTube?

The built-in neural voices are clear and natural enough for short news explainers, with several voices and speeds. They are not a human recording. If you prefer your own voice, export the script and SRT captions and record it yourself.

Where does the footage come from, and can I use it?

From Pixabay or Pexels, through your own key. Both licenses generally allow free use in videos, with their own conditions, so check each provider's terms. Credits are included in the export. Where footage would mislead, the tool uses a clean text card instead.

How long does a video take, and what format is it?

Video is recorded in real time, so a 60 second Short takes about a minute to render. In recent Chrome the file is MP4. Other browsers may produce WebM, which several platforms refuse, so use Chrome or Edge on a desktop.

Does it work on my phone, or in languages other than English?

You can review scripts on a phone, but the video studio downloads a voice model and renders in real time, so use desktop Chrome or Edge for video. Scripts and voices are tuned for English today. Other languages are not supported yet.
